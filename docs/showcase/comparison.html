How it compares

A solver in a market of trackers.

Every major app — MyFitnessPal, MacroFactor, Cronometer, Lose It, Carbon — is built to log what you ate or coach a number. Cut Protocol is built to solve the plan: a full day, week or month scaled to hit your macro target while respecting your diet and exclusions. MyFitnessPal and Lose It do ship meal planners on their paid tiers; the difference is that they suggest recipes from a fixed library, while this scales portions against your exact target — and says so when it can’t hit it.

The market tracks. People want something that plans — and they distrust AI logging enough that a deterministic solver is a selling point, not a limitation.

Where Cut Protocol wins

It plans, it doesn’t just log

The big trackers are built to record what you already ate. Cut Protocol solves the whole day to your target, scales the portions, and tells you when it can’t hit the number instead of quietly missing it.

Allergies are a filter, not a tag

Exclusions are matched across ingredients, recipe titles and step text — majors, species depth and rare allergens — rather than a single diet label. It is a filter, not a safety guarantee; see the caveat below.

Own it, offline, private

One-time, no subscription, no ads, no cloud. Your account and your data live on your own machine.

Honest by design

No red on food, a hard calorie floor, no streak-shaming, and an optional eating-disorder wellbeing check. Calm, not a slot machine.

Where it’s behind (and honest about it)

No quick-logging

It plans what to eat; it doesn’t yet capture what you actually ate by barcode or photo — the market’s #1 convenience.

Desktop only

Most logging happens on a phone. A companion mobile-log is the realistic bridge.

Household & cross-contamination

Solves for one eater today; “may-contain” cross-contamination flags and multi-person plans are the top requested additions.

Allergen caveat. Cut Protocol’s exclusion filter is thorough, but it is not a guarantee. It cannot see cross-contamination, “may contain” warnings, or an ingredient a recipe never named. Always read labels and treat a generated plan as a starting point, not a clearance. This matches the disclaimer shown inside the app.
